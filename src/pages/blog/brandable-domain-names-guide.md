A **brandable domain** is a name people can recognize, remember and associate with a product or business. It does not have to describe every service you offer. It does need to be easy for the people you hope to reach.

A strong brandable name gives you room to grow without making your customers guess how to find you.

## What makes a domain brandable?

Start with these practical qualities:

- **Easy to say:** Can someone pronounce it without hesitation?
- **Easy to type:** Can they spell it after hearing it once?
- **Distinctive:** Will it get confused with competitors?
- **Appropriate:** Does the tone fit the people you serve?
- **Flexible:** Can the name still work if the business changes direction?
- **Reasonably priced:** Will you be comfortable with ongoing renewal costs?

A made-up word can work well if it is intuitive to read and say. A familiar word can also work well if it is not too generic or too close to an existing brand.

## How to brainstorm brandable domains

### Combine familiar ideas

Write down the problem you solve, the experience you want people to feel, and words associated with the market. Experiment with combinations that still sound natural when spoken aloud.

### Shortlist names without judging too early

Give yourself at least 10 possibilities before ruling them out. You may find that the obvious favorite is taken, expensive or confusing.

### Test the names with people

Ask someone to write each name after hearing it. See whether they remember it later. A name that looks beautiful on a screen but is difficult to communicate verbally may cost you referrals.

## Does a brandable name need a .com?

Not necessarily. `.com` is familiar, but other extensions may suit a local market, product category or community.

What matters is that the **complete domain** is clear to your audience. Read our [domain extensions guide](/blog/domain-extensions-guide) before choosing.

## Check the risks before you register

Search for similar businesses, products and trademarks. Check possible confusion with a large brand, particularly in your planned market.

Then use our [domain availability checker](/domain-availability-checker) to test the exact domain. Confirm eligibility, registration pricing and renewal terms with a registrar.

## Brandable versus keyword-rich names

A keyword-rich name may communicate the subject quickly. A distinctive name may be easier to expand into a wider brand. Neither type automatically wins in search engines.

Choose based on your positioning and the user experience, not on a promise of guaranteed ranking performance.

## A five-minute brand test

Give your best three candidates a score from 1 to 5 for memorability, pronunciation, spelling, fit and flexibility. Check registration cost separately. The purpose is to spot obvious weaknesses, not calculate a magic formula for a successful brand.
