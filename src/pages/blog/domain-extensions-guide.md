The extension is the part of a domain that comes after the last dot. In `example.com`, it is `.com`. This is called a **top-level domain**, or TLD.

An extension influences how people interpret your name, but it does not automatically determine whether a website is trustworthy, successful, or likely to rank on search engines.

## General-purpose extensions

- **.com:** Familiar around the world and useful for many commercial or general projects.
- **.org:** Often associated with organizations and communities, though uses and registration rules can vary.
- **.net:** An established extension that can work for general or technology-oriented sites.

A familiar extension can reduce confusion, but a clear name on another suitable extension may still be the right choice.

## Technology and sector-related extensions

Extensions such as `.io`, `.ai` and `.app` appear frequently in technology branding. Do not assume that a sector association makes them inherently better for SEO or guarantees availability.

Some extensions have special technical or registration requirements. For example, check whether HTTPS is required by the browser ecosystem and whether your registrar supports the extension.

## Country-code domains

Country-code TLDs such as `.ng` and `.uk` may signal a country or regional audience. Some have specific residency or eligibility requirements, while others have broader registration options.

If your audience is local, a relevant country extension can be useful. If your business serves several regions, consider whether the same name will be clear internationally.

## Which extension should you pick?

Ask:

1. Will visitors recognize and remember the full domain?
2. Does the extension fit where the audience is?
3. Are there restrictions on who can register or use it?
4. What are the **renewal and transfer fees**, not just introductory prices?
5. Could people mistakenly type another version of the name?
6. Does the complete domain resemble someone else's brand or trademark?

## Does a .com automatically rank higher?

No extension alone is a shortcut to high rankings. Search engines consider many signals related to the website and its content. Choose for your audience, brand clarity and operational needs, not a promised ranking advantage.

## Can I switch extensions later?

You can build on a different domain, but changing names requires careful redirects, updates to internal links, marketing materials, email addresses and search engine signals. Planning early can save time later.
