A **domain name** is the address people type into a browser to reach a website. For example, in `example.com`, the whole phrase is the domain name. It is easier to remember than the numerical addresses computers use to find one another.

You can own a domain without having a website yet. You can also build a website before deciding on its final name. The domain and the website are connected, but they are not the same thing.

## What are the parts of a domain name?

Take `example.com`:

- **example** is the name you choose.
- **.com** is the top-level domain (TLD), sometimes called the domain extension.
- A full address like `www.example.com` can include a **subdomain** such as `www`.

Other extensions include `.org`, `.net`, `.ng`, `.co.uk`, `.io`, and `.ai`. Rules, prices, and eligibility vary by extension. A country-code extension may serve a particular market, but do not assume every extension is open to everyone.

## How does a domain connect to a website?

When someone visits a website, the Domain Name System (DNS) helps their device find the service linked to the domain. Think of DNS as directions, not the website itself.

A simple journey looks like this:

1. You register a domain through a registrar.
2. You choose where the website will be hosted or published.
3. You configure the domain's DNS records or nameservers as instructed by your hosting provider.
4. After DNS updates propagate, visitors can reach the website using the domain.

The same domain can also support branded email addresses, depending on which services and DNS records you configure.

## Who controls domain names?

You usually register a domain through a **registrar**. The relevant **registry** operates the extension, while the registrar handles the registration service you use. Organizations such as ICANN coordinate important parts of the domain naming system.

Registering a name does not mean you purchased it permanently. Domain registrations are time-limited, usually renewable, and subject to the relevant registration agreement.

## How much does a domain cost?

There is no single price for every extension or every name. The final cost depends on the registrar, extension, possible premium classification, registration period and optional extras.

Before checkout, compare:

- Initial registration price **and renewal price**.
- Transfer costs and any restrictions.
- Privacy or registration-data options where available.
- Whether auto-renewal is enabled.
- Any eligibility requirements for the extension.

A very low first-year offer is not enough to judge the long-term cost.

## Domain name vs website vs hosting

A domain is the **address**. Hosting provides the **place where your website runs or its files are served**. The website is the actual pages, images and functionality visitors use.

For a more detailed comparison, read [domain name vs web hosting](/blog/domain-name-vs-web-hosting).

## How do I choose my first domain?

Start with a name customers can say and spell. Check whether an existing business has a similar name or trademark. Think about whether the name still works if your business grows.

Then use the [domain availability checker](/domain-availability-checker) to test your exact name. A result is only a first check: registration can change, and your registrar must confirm the final availability and terms.
