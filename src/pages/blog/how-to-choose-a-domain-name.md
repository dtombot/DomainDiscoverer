A good domain name should make life easier for the person who hears it, types it and remembers it. You do not need a clever trick or a perfect keyword match. You need a name that fits the project and stands up to everyday use.

Here is a straightforward process you can use before registering your business, blog, product or personal website domain.

## Step 1: Write down what the name needs to do

Ask three questions: Who is it for? What does the project do? Will the name still make sense if it expands?

For a local business, a place-based name can be helpful. For a company planning to serve other regions, a narrow location in the name may eventually feel limiting. Neither is automatically better.

## Step 2: Build a shortlist, not just one option

Start with 10 to 15 candidates. Include some that describe the offer and some that could become distinctive brands.

Try these methods:

- Pair two familiar words that sound natural together.
- Use a short, pronounceable invented name.
- Include a clear service or market term only when it improves understanding.
- Remove filler words that make the name longer without adding meaning.

Don't rush to buy the first available option.

## Step 3: Pass the speaking and spelling test

Say the name aloud to someone who has not seen it. Ask them to type it. If they need repeated clarification, the name may create friction in referrals, podcast mentions and conversations.

Avoid ambiguous spellings, unnecessary hyphens and numbers unless they are meaningful to the brand. A slightly longer clear name can be more useful than a short name nobody can spell.

## Step 4: Choose the extension deliberately

`.com` is familiar to many users, but it is not the only valid choice. Country-code extensions can fit local audiences. Other extensions can express a sector or product category.

Compare extensions using your actual audience, eligibility, price, renewal costs and potential confusion with similar existing names. For more detail, see our [guide to domain extensions](/blog/domain-extensions-guide).

## Step 5: Check for naming conflicts

A domain showing as available does **not** mean the name is legally safe to use.

Search for businesses, products and trademarks with similar names in the markets where you intend to operate. Check the relevant trademark databases and seek qualified legal advice if the brand is important or the risk is unclear.

Also check social profiles and common misspellings so you know how the name will look outside a browser.

## Step 6: Check availability, then confirm at checkout

Use our [free domain availability checker](/domain-availability-checker) for a quick first check.

A lookup is not a reservation. Prices and eligibility can change, and some domains are reserved or premium-priced. Confirm everything with the registrar before paying.

## Step 7: Compare the total ownership cost

Look at the second year as well as the first. Verify renewal pricing, transfer policies, registration term, auto-renewal, DNS management and any privacy options.

Keep the login and recovery email for your registrar account secure. Where supported, enable multi-factor authentication.

## Step 8: Make a final choice

Score each shortlisted name from 1 to 5 for **clarity, memorability, brand fit, flexibility, cost and potential conflicts**. The score is not scientific; it simply makes trade-offs visible.

Pick the name that feels clear and practical after these checks, rather than the one with the flashiest sales pitch.
