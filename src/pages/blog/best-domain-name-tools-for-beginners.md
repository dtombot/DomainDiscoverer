You do not need a paid stack of software to choose your first domain name. Start with one reliable availability checker, a registrar where you can review the final cost, and a way to keep your shortlist organized.

Additional tools can help if you are buying an existing domain or researching names for resale, but they are not a requirement for a new website.

## Start with a domain availability checker

Our [free domain checker](/domain-availability-checker) tests one name per submission. This is useful when you have narrowed your ideas to a manageable shortlist.

When the name appears available, confirm it at the registrar. You can also compare alternatives with an external service such as [Instant Domain Search](https://instantdomainsearch.com/).

## Compare registrars carefully

A registrar is the company through which you register and manage a domain. Examples worth comparing include Namecheap, Porkbun and Cloudflare Registrar. This is not a price ranking, and eligibility and supported extensions vary by company.

Before you register, compare renewal fees, supported extensions, transfer terms, DNS features and privacy options. Cheap first-year pricing is only part of the picture.

## Use a sales database when buying premium or resale names

[NameBio](https://namebio.com/) publishes reported historical domain sales. It can help you understand what certain name patterns have sold for, though no previous sale establishes the value of your particular name.

For a personal blog or ordinary new business website, you may not need a paid valuation tool at all.

## Research expired names with extra care

[ExpiredDomains.net](https://www.expireddomains.net/) helps users discover expired and expiring names. These have different availability paths: some are already released, while others may be in auction or still held by their owners.

Before paying for an expired name, inspect its history, existing links and possible trademark risks. A familiar word or an old registration date is not proof of quality.

## Add SEO tools only when relevant

Ahrefs and Semrush provide search and backlink research. They can be useful when evaluating a domain with a previous website or when planning content around a topic.

Treat estimated traffic, keyword volume and proprietary quality scores as signals, not guarantees. Search rankings depend on much more than the name itself.

## Match the tool to your task

| Your question | Starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Is this exact domain available? | Domain Discoverer checker, then registrar checkout |
| What might similar names have sold for? | NameBio |
| Has this domain had previous ownership or use? | DomainIQ and historical research |
| Does the name have suspicious backlinks? | Ahrefs or Semrush |
| Are related names registered across extensions? | DotDB |
| Where can I browse expired names? | ExpiredDomains.net |

Explore our [research tools directory](/tools) for the purpose and limitations of each service.

## A practical beginner workflow

Write 10 names, shortlist three, check each exact extension, then compare renewal cost and obvious naming conflicts. Only go deeper if the domain has an existing history or a significant purchase price.
